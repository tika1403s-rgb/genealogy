## FIELD NODES (13 total)

### 1. **Babylonian Mathematics** (c. 3000-300 BCE)
**Core Focus:** Sexagesimal (base-60) system, place-value notation, astronomical calculations, practical algebra

**Key Characteristics:**
- Place-value positional system (major innovation from Sumerians/Akkadians)
- Advanced computational methods using clay tablets and cuneiform
- Algorithmic approach to solving quadratic and cubic equations
- No zero symbol initially (added ~300 BCE in Seleucid period)
- Extensive mathematical tables for multiplication, reciprocals, squares, cubes
- Pythagorean triples known ~1000 years before Pythagoras

**Applications:** Land surveying, taxation, astronomy, architecture, irrigation engineering

**Sources:** Clay tablets from Old Babylonian period (c. 2000-1600 BCE), particularly Plimpton 322

---

### 2. **Egyptian Mathematics** (c. 3000-300 BCE)
**Core Focus:** Practical geometry, unit fractions, area/volume calculations, surveying

**Key Characteristics:**
- Hieratic numerals (decimal system without place value)
- Unit fractions exclusively (numerator always 1)
- Practical focus on land measurement after Nile flooding
- Formula-based approach without formal proofs
- 2/n tables for fraction decomposition
- Approximation of π ≈ 3.16 from circle calculations
- Seked (slope) calculations for pyramid construction

**Applications:** Architecture (pyramids), agriculture, taxation, resource distribution

**Sources:** Rhind Mathematical Papyrus (c. 1650 BCE), Moscow Mathematical Papyrus (c. 1850 BCE)

---

### 3. **Greek Geometry** (c. 600 BCE - 400 CE)
**Core Focus:** Axiomatic deductive reasoning, rigorous proof, abstract theoretical mathematics

**Key Characteristics:**
- First systematic use of logical deduction and formal proof
- Shift from practical to theoretical mathematics
- Axiomatic method (definitions → postulates → theorems)
- Geometric algebra (solving algebraic problems geometrically)
- Focus on pure mathematics as intellectual pursuit
- Method of exhaustion (precursor to calculus)
- Investigation of incommensurables (irrational numbers)

**Applications:** Initially theoretical; later applied to astronomy, optics, engineering

**Legacy:** Foundation for Western mathematical tradition

---

### 4. **Greek Number Theory** (c. 500 BCE - 300 CE)
**Core Focus:** Properties of integers, primes, perfect numbers, figurate numbers

**Key Contributions:**
- Perfect numbers, amicable numbers
- Prime number infinity proof
- Euclidean algorithm for greatest common divisor
- Theory of proportions and ratios
- Arithmetic/geometric/harmonic means
- Pythagorean number mysticism

**Philosophical Integration:** Numbers seen as fundamental to understanding reality

---

### 5. **Chinese Arithmetic** (c. 1000 BCE - 400 CE)
**Core Focus:** Decimal system, practical algorithms, linear equations, negative numbers

**Key Characteristics:**
- Decimal place-value system with counting rods
- First systematic use of negative numbers (rod calculus)
- Algorithms for solving systems of linear equations (fangcheng method)
- Matrix-like methods predating Western linear algebra by ~1500 years
- Efficient computational techniques
- Magic squares and combinatorics
- Approximations of π

**Applications:** Commerce, taxation, engineering, astronomy, calendar calculations

**Cultural Context:** Mathematics as practical art of calculation (suanshu)

---

### 6. **Chinese Geometry** (c. 500 BCE - 400 CE)
**Core Focus:** Pythagorean theorem (gougu theorem), volume calculations, surveying

**Key Characteristics:**
- Independent discovery of Pythagorean theorem
- Volume formulas for complex solids (pyramids, frustums)
- Surveying techniques (sea island measurements)
- Geometric dissection proofs
- Integration with cosmological concepts

**Sources:** Zhou Bi Suan Jing, Nine Chapters on Mathematical Art

---

### 7. **Indian Numerical Systems** (c. 500 BCE - 400 CE)
**Core Focus:** Place-value notation, zero concept, decimal system

**Revolutionary Contributions:**
- Zero as both placeholder AND number (c. 5th-7th century CE)
- Full decimal place-value system
- Positional notation that enabled modern arithmetic
- Symbol for zero (shunya - void/emptiness)
- Negative number arithmetic rules

**Global Impact:** Via Arabic transmission, became foundation of modern number systems worldwide

---

### 8. **Indian Trigonometry & Astronomy** (c. 400-700 CE)

### 9. **Mayan Mathematics** (c. 300 BCE - 900 CE)
**Core Focus:** Vigesimal (base-20) system, calendrical calculations, independent zero

**Key Characteristics:**
- Base-20 positional notation
- Independent invention of zero (shell glyph) by c. 36 BCE

- Three symbols: dot (1), bar (5), shell (0)

- Lunar cycle calculations (405 lunations = 11,960 days, actual: 11,959.888)

**Applications:** Calendar systems (Tzolk'in, Haab', Long Count), astronomy, ritual timekeeping

### GREEK TRADITION

**4. Thales of Miletus (c. 624-546 BCE)**
- First to introduce deductive reasoning to geometry
- Proved geometric theorems rather than just discovering patterns
- Used similar triangles for indirect measurement (pyramid height, ship distances)

**Key Theorems:**
- Circle bisected by diameter
- Base angles of isosceles triangle are equal
- Vertical angles are equal
- Angle inscribed in semicircle is right angle

**Cultural Impact:** Shifted Greek mathematics from practical to theoretical

**5. Pythagoras of Samos (c. 570-495 BCE)**
- Founded Pythagorean Brotherhood with mathematical-mystical philosophy
- "All is number" - mathematical reality underlying universe

**Attributed Discoveries:**
- Pythagorean theorem (though known to Babylonians earlier)
- Discovery of incommensurables (√2) - challenged Greek mathematical worldview
- Musical intervals based on mathematical ratios
- Sum of angles in triangle = 180°

**Philosophy:** Numbers as divine, eternal truths; mathematics as spiritual discipline

**6. Hippocrates of Chios (c. 470-410 BCE)**
- First to compile Elements of Geometry (now lost, predated Euclid)
- Squared the lune (crescent shape)
- Work on duplication of cube problem

**Contribution:** Early attempt at systematic organization of geometric knowledge

**7. Eudoxus of Cnidus (c. 408-355 BCE)**
- Developed theory of proportions (Book V of Euclid's Elements)
- Method of exhaustion for calculating areas and volumes
- Solved problem of incommensurables through proportion theory

**Major Achievement:** Rigorous foundation for dealing with irrational magnitudes

**8. Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BCE)**
- Compiled and systematized Greek mathematical knowledge in Elements
- Established axiomatic method as mathematical standard
- Teacher at Museum of Alexandria under Ptolemy I

**Elements Structure:**
- 13 books covering plane geometry, number theory, solid geometry
- 5 postulates, 5 common notions (axioms), 465 propositions
- Built entire system through logical deduction from first principles

**Legacy:** Most influential mathematics textbook for 2000 years

**9. Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287-212 BCE)**
- Greatest mathematician of antiquity
- Anticipated integral calculus through method of exhaustion

**Major Contributions:**
- Calculated π between 3.1408 and 3.1429 (using 96-sided polygons)
- Surface area and volume of sphere (4πr², 4/3πr³)
- Areas under parabolas
- Spiral of Archimedes
- Centers of gravity and hydrostatics
- Principle of the lever

**Quote:** "Give me a place to stand, and I shall move the Earth"

**Death:** Killed during Roman siege of Syracuse (212 BCE), allegedly while contemplating geometric problem

**10. Apollonius of Perga (c. 262-190 BCE)**
- "The Great Geometer"
- Author of Conics (8 books, 7 survive in Greek/Arabic)

**Major Work:**
- Systematic treatment of conic sections (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola)
- Coined terms: ellipse, parabola, hyperbola
- Advanced methods of geometric construction
- Theory of epicycles for planetary motion

**Influence:** Renaissance scientific revolution depended heavily on his conic sections

**11. Eratosthenes of Cyrene (c. 276-194 BCE)**
- Calculated Earth's circumference with remarkable accuracy
- Created "Sieve of Eratosthenes" algorithm for finding prime numbers
- Librarian at Alexandria

**Method:** Used sun angle difference between Alexandria and Syene to calculate Earth's size

**12. Hipparchus of Nicaea (c. 190-120 BCE)**
- Father of trigonometry
- Created first trigonometric table (chord function, precursor to sine)
- Discovered precession of equinoxes
- Star catalog with ~850 stars

**13. Diophantus of Alexandria (fl. c. 250 CE)**
- "Father of Algebra"
- Author of Arithmetica (13 books, 10 survive)

**Contributions:**
- First systematic use of algebraic notation
- Indeterminate equations (Diophantine equations)
- Treated numbers abstractly rather than as geometric magnitudes

**Legacy:** Inspired Islamic algebra; Fermat's Last Theorem written in margin of his work

## BREAKTHROUGHS (18 total)

### **1. Place-Value Notation (Babylonian, c. 2000 BCE)**
**Innovation:** Position determines value (e.g., "3" in third position = 3×60²)

**Significance:**
- Enabled compact representation of large numbers
- Facilitated complex calculations
- Foundation for modern positional notation

**Limitation:** Initially no symbol for zero (empty spaces used), added in Seleucid period (~300 BCE)

**Sources:** Clay tablets from Old Babylonian period confirm widespread use

---

### **2. Pythagorean Theorem - Multiple Independent Discoveries**

**Babylonian (c. 1800 BCE):**
- Plimpton 322 tablet shows systematic Pythagorean triples
- Some triples extremely large (e.g., 12,709² + 13,500² = 18,541²)
- Indicates algorithmic generation method

**Chinese (c. 1000 BCE):**
- Zhou Bi Suan Jing presents "gougu theorem"
- Geometric proof using area dissection
- Applied to surveying and astronomy

**Greek (c. 540 BCE):**
- Pythagoras or followers provided formal proof
- Multiple proof methods developed
- Connected to number theory and geometry

**Indian (c. 800 BCE):**
- Baudhayana Sulbasutra states theorem
- Geometric constructions for altars
- Numerical approximations

**Key Insight:** Same mathematical truth discovered independently across civilizations - suggests fundamental nature of geometry

---

### **3. Discovery of Incommensurables (√2 is Irrational) (Greek, c. 500 BCE)**
**Attributed to:** Pythagoreans (possibly Hippasus)

**Proof Method:** Reductio ad absurdum
- Assume √2 = p/q in lowest terms
- Then 2q² = p² → p is even → p = 2k
- Then 2q² = 4k² → q² = 2k² → q is even
- Contradiction: both p and q even, not in lowest terms

**Cultural Crisis:** Shattered Pythagorean belief that "all is number" (rational)

**Long-term Impact:** 
- Forced development of geometric algebra
- Led to Eudoxus's theory of proportions
- Highlighted limits of arithmetic approach

---

### **4. Axiomatic-Deductive Method (Greek, c. 300 BCE)**
**Systematized by:** Euclid in Elements

**Method:**
1. Establish definitions (undefined terms: point, line, plane)
2. State axioms/postulates (self-evident truths)
3. Prove theorems through logical deduction
4. Each theorem becomes foundation for next

**Revolutionary Aspect:** Knowledge built on minimal assumptions through pure logic

**Influence:** Model for all subsequent mathematics and deductive science

---

### **5. Method of Exhaustion (Greek, c. 370 BCE)**
**Developed by:** Eudoxus; perfected by Archimedes

**Technique:**
- Approximate curve with polygons
- Increase polygon sides → better approximation
- Prove result by contradiction (if different, leads to absurdity)

**Applications:**
- Area of circle
- Volume of sphere, cone, pyramid
- Area under parabola

**Historical Significance:** Precursor to integral calculus (1800 years early)

---

### **6. Algorithm for Systematic Problem-Solving (Babylonian, c. 2000 BCE)**
**Nature:** Step-by-step procedures for mathematical calculations

**Examples:**
- Quadratic equation solutions (not expressed algebraically, but procedurally)
- Square root approximations
- Reciprocal calculations

**Innovation:** Mathematics as computation, not just relationships

**Legacy:** Algorithmic thinking fundamental to modern computing

---

### **7. Unit Fraction System (Egyptian, c. 2000 BCE)**
**Method:** Express all fractions as sums of distinct unit fractions (numerator = 1)
- Example: 2/5 = 1/3 + 1/15

**2/n Tables:** Systematic decompositions for even numerators

**Advantages:**
- Simplified sharing/division problems
- Avoided complex fraction arithmetic
- Unique to Egyptian tradition

**Limitations:** Cumbersome for complex calculations

---

### **8. Decimal Place-Value System (Chinese, c. 100 BCE; Indian, c. 5-7th century CE)**

**Chinese Development:**
- Counting rods on grid
- Decimal positional notation
- Facilitated complex calculations
- Rod calculus for negative numbers (red/black rods)

**Indian Development:**
- Full symbolic decimal system with zero
- Place-value notation in written form
- Enabled modern arithmetic

**Combined Impact:** Foundation of modern number system

---

### **9. Zero as Number and Placeholder (Indian, c. 7th century CE; Mayan, c. 36 BCE)**

**Indian Development (Brahmagupta, 628 CE):**
- Zero (shunya) as full number with arithmetic rules
- n + 0 = n
- n × 0 = 0
- n - n = 0
- 0/n = 0
- n/0 = undefined/infinity (debated)

**Mayan Development (independent, c. 36 BCE):**
- Shell glyph representing completion/void
- Essential for Long Count calendar
- Sacred and mathematical significance

**Revolutionary Impact:**
- Enabled full positional notation
- Simplified arithmetic
- Foundation for algebra
- Philosophical implications (representing nothing/void)

---

### **10. Negative Numbers (Chinese, c. 100 BCE; Indian, c. 7th century CE)**

**Chinese Development:**
- Rod calculus with red (positive) and black (negative) rods
- Practical use in solving equations
- Fangcheng method for systems of equations

**Indian Development:**
- Brahmagupta formalized rules:
  - Negative × Negative = Positive
  - Negative × Positive = Negative
  - Debt/loss interpretation

**European Resistance:** Not accepted in Europe until 1400s-1600s

---

### **11. Systematic Trigonometry (Indian, c. 499 CE)**
**Innovator:** Aryabhata

**Breakthrough:** Sine function (jya) as geometric relationship

**Development:**
- Trigonometric tables
- Half-chord function (precursor to modern sine)
- Used for astronomical calculations

**Greek Precedent:** Chord function (Hipparchus, ~140 BCE) - related but different approach

---

### **12. Matrix Methods for Linear Equations (Chinese, c. 100 BCE)**
**Method:** Fangcheng (rectangular arrays) in Nine Chapters

**Technique:**
- Coefficients arranged in rectangular pattern
- Systematic elimination (Gaussian elimination 1500 years early)
- Rod calculus for calculations

**Example Problem:** System of 3 equations, 3 unknowns solved algorithmically

**Western Rediscovery:** Gauss (1810s), but Chinese method more systematic

---

### **13. Approximation of π**

**Babylonian:** π ≈ 3.125 (c. 2000 BCE)
**Egyptian:** π ≈ 3.16 (from (16/9)² formula for circle area, c. 1650 BCE)
**Archimedes:** 3.1408 < π < 3.1429 (96-gon method, c. 250 BCE)
**Liu Hui:** π ≈ 3.1416 (96-gon, similar method to Archimedes, 263 CE)
**Zu Chongzhi:** 3.1415926 < π < 3.1415927 (24,576-gon, c. 480 CE) [*slightly beyond period*]

**Progression shows:** Increasing precision through method of exhaustion across cultures

---

### **14. Conic Sections Theory (Greek, c. 350-200 BCE)**
**Menaechmus:** Discovered while attempting to duplicate cube

**Apollonius (c. 200 BCE):**
- Systematic treatment in Conics (8 books)
- Unified approach: single cone, varying plane angle
- Named ellipse, parabola, hyperbola
- 387 propositions

**Applications:**
- Planetary motion (epicycles)
- Optics (parabolic mirrors)
- Renaissance astronomy (Kepler's orbits)

---

### **15. Vigesimal Positional System with Zero (Mayan, c. 300 BCE - 36 BCE)**
**Base-20 System:**
- Three symbols: dot (1), bar (5), shell (0)
- Vertical place notation
- Modified for calendar: 20, 360, 7200... (instead of pure 20, 400, 8000...)

**Zero Development:**
- Independent of Old World
- Earlier documented use than Indian (though both ancient)
- Sacred and mathematical significance

---

### **16. Calendar Mathematics (Multiple Traditions)**

**Egyptian:**
- 365-day civil calendar
- Solar year recognition

**Babylonian:**
- Lunisolar calendar
- Intercalation methods
- Astronomical predictions

**Chinese:**
- Complex lunisolar calculations
- 19-year Metonic cycle

**Mayan:**
- Tzolk'in (260 days)
- Haab' (365 days)
- Long Count (historical dates)
- Calendar Round (52-year cycle)
- Year length: 365.2420 days (error: 0.0002!)

### **17. Proof by Contradiction (Reductio ad Absurdum) (Greek, c. 500 BCE)**
**Method:**
1. Assume opposite of what you want to prove
2. Derive logical contradiction
3. Conclude original statement must be true

**Classic Examples:**
- √2 is irrational (Pythagoreans)
- Infinitely many primes (Euclid)
- Method of exhaustion proofs (Archimedes)

**Impact:** Became fundamental proof technique in mathematics

---

### **18. Theory of Proportions (Greek, c. 370 BCE)**
**Eudoxus's Achievement:** Rigorous treatment of ratios between incommensurable magnitudes

**Problem Solved:** How to compare geometric magnitudes when they're not rational multiples of each other

**Book V of Elements:**
- Definition of same ratio
- Operations with proportions
- Geometric foundation for real numbers (avant la lettre)

**Historical Impact:** Allowed Greek mathematics to progress despite irrational numbers

### GREEK

**5. Elements by Euclid (c. 300 BCE)**
**Structure:**
- 13 books
- ~465 propositions
- Systematic development from axioms

**Content by Book:**
- Books I-IV: Plane geometry
- Book V: Theory of proportions (Eudoxus)
- Book VI: Similar figures
- Books VII-IX: Number theory
  * Book VII: GCD algorithm
  * Book IX: Infinitude of primes
- Book X: Irrational magnitudes (classification)
- Books XI-XIII: Solid geometry
  * Book XIII: Platonic solids

**5 Postulates:**
1. Straight line between two points
2. Line extends indefinitely
3. Circle with any center and radius
4. All right angles equal
5. Parallel postulate (most complex)

**5 Common Notions (Axioms):**
1. Things equal to same thing are equal
2. Equals added to equals produce equals
3. Equals subtracted from equals produce equals
4. Things coinciding are equal
5. Whole greater than part

**Legacy:**
- Most published book after Bible for centuries
- Used as geometry textbook into 1900s
- Model for axiomatic method

**6. On the Sphere and Cylinder by Archimedes (c. 225 BCE)**
**Major Results:**
- Surface area of sphere = 4πr²
- Volume of sphere = 4/3 πr³
- Sphere:cylinder ratio (volume and surface) = 2:3

**Method:** Exhaustion with inscribed/circumscribed solids

**Archimedes's Pride:** Requested cylinder circumscribing sphere be on his tomb

**7. Measurement of a Circle by Archimedes (c. 250 BCE)**
**Content:**
- Proposition 1: Area of circle = triangle with base=circumference, height=radius
- Proposition 3: π calculation using polygons
- 3 10/71 < π < 3 1/7

**Method:** Inscribed and circumscribed polygons up to 96 sides

**8. The Sand Reckoner by Archimedes (c. 235 BCE)**
**Purpose:** Show any number, however large, can be represented

**Example:** Number of sand grains to fill universe

**Innovation:** Large number notation system

- Myriad-myriads, and higher orders

**Significance:** Extended concept of numerical representation

**9. Conics by Apollonius (c. 200 BCE)**
**Structure:** 8 books (Book 8 lost; Books 5-7 survive only in Arabic)

**Content:**
- Books I-II: Generation of conics; fundamental properties
- Book III: Tangent properties; pole/polar theory
- Book IV: Intersection of conics
- Book V: Normals; maximum/minimum lines
- Book VI-VII: Equal and similar conics

**Innovation:**
- Unified approach (one cone, varying plane angle)

- Systematic treatment of all properties

**Legacy:** Essential for Kepler's astronomy, projectile motion, optics

## KEY THEMES TO EXPLORE

### **1. Multiple Independent Discoveries**
**Pythagorean Theorem:** Found independently in Babylon (~1800 BCE), China (~1000 BCE), India (~800 BCE), Greece (~540 BCE)
- **Babylonian:** Computational/tabular approach (Plimpton 322)
- **Chinese:** Geometric proof by dissection (gougu)
- **Indian:** Geometric construction (Sulbasutras)
- **Greek:** Formal proof tradition (Pythagoreans)

**Implication:** Fundamental truths discovered independently suggest universal nature of mathematical reality

**Zero:**
- **Indian:** Brahmagupta (628 CE) - number with arithmetic
- **Mayan:** Shell glyph (36 BCE documented) - placeholder and sacred symbol
- **Babylonian:** Late development (~300 BCE) - placeholder only

**Approximations of π:** 
- Different methods, converging accuracy across civilizations
- Shows universal drive to understand circular measurement

---

### **2. Practical vs. Theoretical Mathematics**

**Practical Traditions (Egyptian, Babylonian, Chinese, Mayan):**
- **Focus:** Solving real-world problems
- **Method:** Algorithms, procedures, rules
- **Applications:** Surveying, commerce, astronomy, architecture, taxation
- **Justification:** "It works" - empirical verification
- **Transmission:** Scribal schools, master-apprentice

**Theoretical Tradition (Greek):**
- **Focus:** Understanding mathematical truth
- **Method:** Proof, logical deduction
- **Applications:** Secondary; mathematics valued for own sake
- **Justification:** Rigorous demonstration from axioms
- **Philosophy:** Mathematics as path to eternal truths

**Hybrid (Indian, Later Chinese):**
- **Indian:** Practical astronomy with theoretical foundations
- **Liu Hui's Commentary:** Added rigorous proofs to practical Chinese algorithms
- **Synthesis:** Effectiveness of computation with understanding of why

**Modern Legacy:** Science requires both approaches - computational power and theoretical understanding

---

### **3. Numerical Notation Systems and Computational Implications**

**Egyptian (Hieratic/Hieroglyphic):**
- Decimal but non-positional
- Unit fractions only
- **Limitation:** Complex calculations difficult
- **Strength:** Adequate for practical administration

**Babylonian (Sexagesimal):**
- Base-60 positional
- **Advantage:** Many divisors (1,2,3,4,5,6,10,12,15,20,30,60)
- **Limitation:** No zero initially; context-dependent place value
- **Legacy:** Time (60 seconds/minutes), angles (360°)

**Chinese (Decimal with Counting Rods):**
- Base-10 positional on counting board
- Empty space for zero
- Red/black for positive/negative
- **Advantage:** Facilitated matrix methods, complex computation
- **Limitation:** Computational device, not written

**Indian (Decimal Place-Value with Zero):**
- Full symbolic system
- Zero as number and placeholder
- **Revolutionary:** Enabled modern arithmetic
- **Global Impact:** Via Arabic transmission to Europe

**Mayan (Vigesimal):**
- Base-20 positional
- Independent zero invention
- Modified for calendar (360 instead of 400 in third position)
- **Strength:** Large number calculations for astronomy
- **Context:** Primarily calendrical-astronomical

**Key Insight:** Notation system shapes mathematical possibilities
- Positional notation enables complex calculation
- Symbolic zero transforms arithmetic
- Choice of base reflects practical concerns (fingers/toes, divisibility)

---

### **4. Transmission Between Traditions (Limited in Ancient Period)**

**Within Regions:**
- **Mesopotamia:** Sumerian → Akkadian → Babylonian → Seleucid
- **Egypt:** Continuous tradition ~2000 years
- **Greece:** Ionian → Athenian → Alexandrian
- **India:** Vedic → Classical → Golden Age
- **China:** Continuous tradition across dynasties

**Limited Cross-Cultural Exchange:**
- **Greek-Egyptian:** Greeks studied in Egypt (Thales, Pythagoras allegedly)
  * But fundamental approaches remained distinct
- **Indian-Chinese:** Buddhist transmission brought some Indian astronomy/mathematics to China
  * Limited in ancient period, increased medieval
- **Greek-Babylonian:** Greeks acquired astronomical data
  * But computational methods less transferred

**Isolated Developments:**
- **Mayan:** Completely independent of Old World
  * No contact until 1500s CE
  * Parallel innovations (zero, positional notation)
- **Chinese:** Largely independent development
  * Some Indian influence via Buddhism (later period)

**Barriers to Transmission:**
- Language differences
- Cultural isolation
- Different philosophical frameworks
- Limited travel and communication
- Different notation systems

**Later Medieval Synthesis:**
- Islamic scholars (800-1300 CE) synthesized:
  * Greek geometry + Indian arithmetic + Babylonian astronomy
- Enabled European Renaissance mathematics
- But this is post-ancient period

**Key Pattern:** Mathematical truth discovered independently, but methods and notation varied by culture

---

### **5. Mathematics and Worldview**

**Pythagorean Philosophy:**
- "All is number" - mathematical order underlies reality
- Musical harmony from numerical ratios
- Celestial harmony (music of spheres)
- Crisis when √2 proved irrational

**Indian Cosmology:**
- Mathematics tied to astronomy (jyotisha)
- Zero concept linked to void/emptiness (shunyata) in Buddhist/Hindu philosophy
- Time cycles requiring vast numbers

**Mayan Worldview:**
- Mathematics inseparable from calendar and ritual
- Zero as completion/beginning
- Time as divine reality requiring precise calculation
- Venus cycles tied to warfare and sacrifice

**Chinese Tradition:**
- Mathematics as practical art (suanshu)
- Less metaphysical than Greek or Indian
- But Liu Hui integrated Confucian/Daoist philosophy in commentaries

**Implications:** Mathematics both universal and culturally embedded
- Same truths, different frameworks
- Applications reflect cultural priorities
